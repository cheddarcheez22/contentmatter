Where diners decide: Maps, Instagram, delivery apps, and their own community

By ContentMatter · 3 min read · Published September 15, 2026 · Updated September 30, 2026

Diners rarely rely on one source. They ask people they know, search online, scan photos, and read reviews, often in the same ten minutes. The goal is to be visible and consistent in each place that matters.

Recommendations still lead

BentoBox's Diner Discovery Report, which surveyed 2,000 diners and was covered by Restaurant Dive in 2023, found that 89% use in person recommendations to find new restaurants and 86% use online research. Among social media users, 56% find restaurants through recommendations from people they know. Your regulars are your best discovery channel, so make it easy for them to share.

Search engines carry online discovery

In the same report, 61% of diners who discover restaurants online use search engines, compared with 27% who use delivery platforms and 22% who use reservation platforms. Search comes first, so your Google listing, photos, and menu need to be accurate and current.

Social media reaches younger diners

Instagram is used for restaurant discovery by 54% of Gen Z and 52% of millennials in that survey, and TikTok by 43% of Gen Z. Short videos of dishes, the kitchen, and the room give younger diners a reason to try you.

Reviews and details close the sale

BrightLocal's 2025 Consumer Search Behavior survey found that 67% of consumers often or always read reviews after a local search, 49% plan a route to the business, and 85% say contact information and hours are important. A review count, correct hours, and a working phone number do much of the closing.

Learn how your community finds you

Ask new guests how they heard about you at the table or in a post visit message. Different communities lean on different places, from family group chats to neighborhood social pages. Your own answers beat any average.

An illustrative example

A group of friends is deciding where to eat on Friday. One person posts a photo in a chat and asks for ideas. Someone replies with a place they visited. The group opens Google Maps, checks the reviews, glances at the menu, and calls to ask about a table for six. At least four of your touchpoints were involved: a friend's recommendation, your listing, your reviews, and your phone.

Common mistakes to avoid

Focusing on one platform and ignoring the rest, letting menus on third party sites go out of date, posting on social media without a clear action, and forgetting to ask regulars to share. The visit usually depends on the weakest link in that chain.

A simple plan for the next 30 days

Week one, search for your restaurant by name and by cuisine and neighborhood, and fix anything wrong on your Google listing. Week two, upload fresh photos of your best dishes and your dining room, and update your menu everywhere it appears. Week three, ask for reviews from guests who enjoyed their meal, and reply to the ones you already have. Week four, post a short video on the platform your guests use and ask your regulars to share it. Review the answers from new guests about how they found you and adjust your focus.

Try this first

Ask your next ten new guests how they found you, and write down the answers. Compare them to where you are spending your time.
