How to win more direct orders: bring delivery regulars to your own link

By ContentMatter · 3 min read · Published November 4, 2026

Delivery apps bring in orders, but they also take a commission and keep the guest's details. Moving even a share of your regulars to your own ordering link keeps more of each sale and lets you reach those guests again.

Know what each order costs

DoorDash's published Marketplace pricing lists the commission on delivery orders at 15% on the Basic plan, 25% on Plus, and 30% on Premier, with pickup at 6% on every plan. Check the current terms before you decide, because plans and rates change. Ordering through your own site or app swaps that commission for a payment processing fee. DoorDash's own pricing page, for example, describes its Online Ordering product as commission free with a processing fee.

Many guests prefer to order from you

NCR Voyix's 2025 Customer Experience Report, based on a November 2024 survey of U.S. consumers with a margin of error of plus or minus 4%, found that 58% of customers prefer to use a restaurant's own app or website to order delivery. The top reasons were convenience at 65%, easy customization at 50%, and earning loyalty points at 36%, as reported by Restaurant Dive. The survey came from a software vendor, so treat it as a signal, not a promise about your neighborhood.

Make your own link easy to find

Put your ordering link in your Google Business Profile, your website header, your Instagram bio, and your email signature. Print a QR code on menus, table cards, and receipts. A guest who has to hunt for your link will open the app instead.

Give a reason to switch

Convenience and loyalty points rank high in that survey, so lead with them instead of a deep discount. A simple points program, a free item after a set number of orders, or first access to new menu items gives a guest a reason to start at your link. Add a card with a QR code to every pickup or delivery bag.

Collect contact details

Every direct order gives you an email address or phone number. That list is the starting point for the follow up we describe in our post on why repeat guests matter.

Count the shift

Track direct orders as a share of all online orders each month, and compare average order size on each channel. If the direct share rises, your link is working. If it is flat, change one thing at a time.

An illustrative example

Say a restaurant gets 100 app delivery orders a week with an average subtotal of $40 and pays a 25% commission. That is $10 an order, or $1,000 a week. If 10 of those guests order direct instead, the restaurant avoids $100 a week in commission, before payment processing fees. These are made up round numbers to show the math. Use your own subtotal and plan rate.

Common mistakes to avoid

Offering a discount so deep that regulars wait for it, hiding the ordering link on the website, using an ordering page that is slow on a phone, and dropping a delivery app before direct orders are steady.

A simple plan for the next 30 days

Week one, pull last month's orders by channel and add up the commissions you paid. Week two, confirm your ordering link works on a phone and add it to your Business Profile, website, and Instagram. Week three, launch a simple reward and put a QR card in every bag. Week four, count direct orders and new contacts, then decide what to adjust.

Try this first

Add up last month's third party commissions. That number is the most you can win back by moving regulars to your own link.

Sources
  • DoorDash for Merchants, Commission Rates and Pricing (accessed November 2026)
  • Restaurant Dive, Most customers prefer ordering delivery direct from restaurants, citing the NCR Voyix 2025 Customer Experience Report (Littman, 2025)
