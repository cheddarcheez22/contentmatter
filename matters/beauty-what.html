What to track: consultations, bookings, calls, and cost per new client

By ContentMatter · 2 min read · Published September 21, 2026 · Updated September 30, 2026

The number that matters is the cost of a new booked client, not the cost of a click. To get it, you need to track calls, forms, and bookings back to the ad or listing that produced them.

Track the first contact

Google's Business Profile performance report counts calls, directions requests, website clicks, messages, and bookings when your appointments run through a booking provider. It also shows the search terms people used to find you. These are free and a good baseline for your practice.

Google Ads can also record calls from your ads and from your website, and can count only calls that last longer than a time you set. That keeps short or accidental calls out of your lead count.

Separate inquiries from consultations

An inquiry is not a consultation, and a consultation is not a treatment. Track each stage: inquiry, booked consultation, attended consultation, and paid treatment. The drop between stages tells you whether the problem is your ad, your phone handling, or your follow up.

Do the cost per new client math

Divide your total marketing spend for a period by the number of new clients who booked in that period. Then compare by source. A channel that produces fewer inquiries but more booked clients is often the better buy.

Keep the information correct

MyAdvice's survey found that 55% of respondents would be less likely to choose a med spa if they found incorrect information on its local search profile or website. Accurate hours, services, and phone numbers are part of the tracking job, because they decide whether a lead becomes a booking.

Protect client privacy

Clinics should be careful about what information is sent to advertising platforms. Avoid passing health details into ad or analytics tools, and confirm your approach with your compliance advisor.

An illustrative example

A med spa spends money on ads and reports 120 leads in a month. When it tracks stages, it finds that 40 became booked consultations, 25 attended, and 12 purchased a treatment. The cost per new client is much higher than the cost per lead suggested. More important, the biggest drop was between inquiry and booking, which points to phone handling, not the ad.

Common mistakes to avoid

Reporting leads without outcomes, mixing new and returning clients in the same count, sending sensitive details to ad tools, and comparing channels without looking at conversion between stages. The cheapest lead is not always the best client.

A simple plan for the next 30 days

Week one, define the stages you will count: inquiry, booked consultation, attended consultation, and paid treatment. Week two, set up call and form tracking with source labels, taking care to keep client health details out of ad tools. Week three, ask the front desk to record the source of every new booking in one place. Week four, calculate the cost per new client by source and look at the drop between stages. Use that drop to decide whether to fix the ad, the phone process, or the follow up.

Try this first

For last month, write down inquiries, booked consultations, and new clients by source. Divide spend by new clients to find your cost per new client.
