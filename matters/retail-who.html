Who walks in: locals, tourists, and gift shoppers

By ContentMatter · 2 min read · Published September 30, 2026

A neighbor, a visitor, and a gift shopper all enter the same door for different reasons. Naming each group helps you decide what to say and where.

Locals are your base

Regular local customers care about convenience, hours, and familiarity. They respond to new arrivals, loyalty perks, and reasons to come back. Email, text messages, and Google Business Profile updates reach them cheaply.

Visitors decide quickly

If your store is near a tourist area, visitors may be a meaningful share of your foot traffic. They use Maps, search on their phones, and decide quickly, so photos, hours, and reviews matter even more for them.

Gift shoppers have a deadline

Someone buying a gift wants to know what to buy, whether you have it, and whether it will arrive or be ready in time. Make popular gift items easy to find, show prices clearly, and state pickup or delivery cutoffs. The NRF notes that consumers are watching their budgets, so options at several price points help.

Match the channel to the shopper

Locals tend to find you through repeat contact and local search. Visitors come through Maps and reviews. Gift shoppers search by product and occasion. Build a short message and one action for each, and see which one grows.

Ask and learn

Use a quick question at checkout: local, visiting, or buying a gift? Tally the answers for a month. The mix may surprise you and can reshape your plan.

An illustrative example

A shop near a busy shopping street sees a wave of first time customers every summer weekend, many holding phones and asking about local specialties. Its profile includes clear photos, popular items, and a note about shipping for visitors who cannot carry things home. Locals are served by a monthly email about new arrivals. The same store, two different messages, two different results.

Common mistakes to avoid

Treating all shoppers as locals, forgetting that visitors need shipping or gift wrap options, assuming a tourist cannot become a repeat customer, and skipping the checkout question. A simple tally often reveals the real mix.

A simple plan for the next 30 days

Week one, start the checkout question and tally local, visitor, and gift. Week two, write a short message for each group, including a note about shipping or gift options for visitors. Week three, update your Business Profile photos and descriptions to match what each group wants to see. Week four, review the tally and see which group grew or shrank after your changes. Use the results to decide where to spend your next marketing dollar. The goal is not a perfect profile, only enough clarity to say the right thing to the right person.

Try this first

At checkout this week, ask each customer whether they are a local, a visitor, or buying a gift. Tally the answers and compare them to where you spend your marketing time.
