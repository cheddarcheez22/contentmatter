How to cut no shows: reminders that keep the chair full

By ContentMatter · 3 min read · Published November 4, 2026

An empty chair costs the same to run as a full one. Reminders, easy rescheduling, and a clear policy are the cheapest ways to keep booked appointments on the calendar.

Reminders work

A Cochrane review by Gurol-Urganci and colleagues pooled eight randomized trials with 6,615 participants. In the seven trials that compared a text reminder with no reminder, covering 5,841 people, 78.6% of people who received a text attended their appointment, compared with 67.8% of people who received none. The review rated this evidence as moderate quality.

Text reminders worked about as well as phone call reminders, and two studies found the cost per attended appointment was 55% and 65% lower for texts. One caution: the trials were about healthcare appointments, not salons or spas, and the authors said the evidence was not yet enough to settle policy. It is a useful guide for clinics and a reasonable test for beauty businesses.

Remind at two moments

Send a confirmation when the booking is made and a reminder about a day before. Include the date, time, address, and one tap links to confirm or reschedule. Add short prep notes for treatments that need them.

Make rescheduling easier than skipping

Many guests miss because cancelling feels awkward. A link to move the appointment, plus a short waitlist of guests who want an earlier slot, can turn a gap into a rebooked chair.

Set a clear policy

Say plainly how much notice you need and what happens if a guest cancels late or does not come. Some businesses take a deposit for longer treatments, and others charge a late cancellation fee. Whatever you choose, show it at booking and in the reminder, apply it the same way every time, and check the rules for your state and license.

Measure your no show rate

Divide missed appointments by booked appointments for the month. Track it by service and by weekday, since longer treatments and slow weekdays often behave differently. Review it monthly and change one thing at a time.

An illustrative example

A clinic books 80 appointments in a month and 8 are missed, a 10% no show rate. After adding a text reminder and a reschedule link, it misses 5 the next month. That is 3 more filled chairs. These are made up round numbers to show the calculation, not results to expect.

Common mistakes to avoid

Sending reminders with no way to reschedule, hiding the cancellation policy until a guest is charged, applying the policy to some guests and not others, and never counting how many appointments are missed.

A simple plan for the next 30 days

Week one, count last month's booked and missed appointments and write down your policy. Week two, set up a confirmation and a day before reminder with a reschedule link. Week three, start a waitlist for earlier openings. Week four, recalculate your no show rate and compare it with week one. Repeat the check each month.

Try this first

Count last month's booked and missed appointments. That one percentage is your baseline.

Sources
  • Cochrane, Mobile phone messaging reminders for attendance at healthcare appointments (Gurol-Urganci et al., 2013)
