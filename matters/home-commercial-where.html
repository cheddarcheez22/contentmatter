Where customers look for a plumber or electrician: Maps, local ads, and your Business Profile

By ContentMatter · 3 min read · Published September 4, 2026 · Updated September 30, 2026

Customers looking for a local service start on Google, and many go straight to Maps. Your Business Profile, your search ads, and your reviews are the three places that decide whether they call you or someone else.

Google is the default starting point

BrightLocal's 2025 Consumer Search Behavior survey of 1,000 US adults found that 61% default to Google for general searches. For local searches, 45% default to Google, 15% to Google Maps, and 14% to social media as a group. Your first job is to be visible and accurate where most of those searches happen.

Your Business Profile is your storefront

In the same survey, 85% of consumers said contact information and opening hours are important when choosing a local business, and 46% rated them very important. Another 56% often or always check that a business's information is accurate and consistent across sources.

Claim and verify your Google Business Profile, then keep your phone number, hours, service areas, and categories identical everywhere your business appears. A wrong phone number on one listing costs you calls you will never know you lost.

Reviews finish the decision

BrightLocal also found that 67% of consumers often or always read business reviews after a local search. For service work, where customers are inviting you into their homes, reviews work like references. Ask every satisfied customer, and reply to the ones you receive.

Consider pay per lead ads

Google's Local Services Ads charge per valid lead instead of per click. Valid leads can include an answered call, a missed call during business hours where the customer stays on the line more than 20 seconds, a message, a voicemail, or a booking request. You set a weekly budget, and once you reach the monthly maximum your ad stops showing, so plan the budget around your busy months.

An illustrative example

A homeowner with a leaking pipe searches on a phone. The first thing on the screen is a row of ads, then a map with three listings, then organic results. The homeowner taps a listing with a nearby address, a rating, and a call button. The business that appears in that map row with accurate hours and recent reviews gets the call. A stronger website would not have changed that outcome.

Common mistakes to avoid

Leaving old phone numbers on directory listings, ignoring reviews, setting a service area that does not match where your team actually works, and putting all of the effort into the website while the Business Profile sits unfinished. Small gaps in the listing often cost more than big gaps on the site.

A simple plan for the next 30 days

Week one, audit your Business Profile for name, phone, hours, service area, and categories, and match them everywhere else. Week two, add current photos of your team, vehicles, and finished work. Week three, ask your last twenty customers for a review with a direct link and reply to every existing review. Week four, compare your listing with the top three competitors in your area and decide whether to test a pay per lead campaign. Repeat the audit each quarter, because small changes happen without anyone noticing.

Try this first

Search for your own service and city on a phone. Check that your listing shows the right number, hours, and service area, then count your reviews against the top three competitors.
