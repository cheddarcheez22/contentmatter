Why reviews matter before a booking

By ContentMatter · 3 min read · Published September 18, 2026 · Updated September 30, 2026

Beauty and clinic clients rarely book the first name they see. BrightLocal reports that 67% of consumers often or always read business reviews after a local search, so your reviews act as your first impression.

Clients compare before they choose

A survey of 275 people who researched med spas online, reported by MyAdvice, found that 63% compare between two and five med spas before deciding. That means your business is rarely the only one under consideration, and reviews are one of the few things clients can compare side by side.

Low ratings close the door

BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey (2023 edition, 1,117 US consumers) found that 87% would not consider a business with an average rating below 3 stars, and that 38% expect a minimum average of 4 stars. A handful of unanswered complaints can cost more bookings than most owners expect.

Trust is earned with detail

BrightLocal's 2025 edition found that 42% of consumers trust online reviews as much as personal recommendations, down from 79% in 2020, and that long, detailed reviews are becoming more influential. Specific reviews that mention the treatment, the provider, and the outcome carry more weight than a star rating alone.

Ask at the right time

In the same survey, email was the most effective way to request a review at 40%, followed by asking in person at 27%. Healthcare and beauty clients generally expect a request within three days to a week after their visit, so a message after the appointment, once the result is visible, tends to land well. Check each review platform's rules before offering any incentive.

Reply to everyone

Consumers prefer businesses that respond to feedback, both positive and negative. Reply calmly and briefly, thank the reviewer, and invite concerns offline. Avoid sharing any client or health details in a public reply.

An illustrative example

Two clinics offer the same treatment at similar prices a few blocks apart. One has fresh reviews that describe the provider by name, the experience, and the result. The other has a few older reviews with no replies. A prospective client who compares them for two minutes is likely to choose the first, even if the second has better equipment. Reviews turn quality into something a stranger can see.

Common mistakes to avoid

Waiting for reviews to arrive on their own, replying with the same canned sentence every time, sharing private details when responding, and asking only your happiest clients. A steady, honest stream of reviews is more believable than a burst.

A simple plan for the next 30 days

Week one, reply to every review that has not received a response, using calm and brief language that does not repeat any private details. Week two, build a short review request email with a direct link and send it to recent clients. Week three, add a gentle in person ask at checkout for clients who say they were pleased. Week four, count new reviews and note what clients mentioned most. Share that language with your team and your website writer, because clients often describe your value better than you do.

Try this first

Send a review request to your last ten clients this week by email. Reply to every review from the last 90 days that has no response.
