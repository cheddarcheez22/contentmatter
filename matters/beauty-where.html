Where clients choose: Google search, reviews, and Instagram

By ContentMatter · 2 min read · Published September 22, 2026 · Updated September 30, 2026

Most clients start with Google, check reviews, and then look at your website. Younger clients lean more on social platforms. Consistency across all of them is what earns the booking.

Google comes first

BrightLocal's 2025 survey found that 61% of consumers default to Google for general searches and 45% default to Google for local searches, with another 15% using Google Maps. Your Business Profile, photos, services, and hours are what they see first.

Younger clients start on social

Among Gen Z respondents, 30% default to Google for local searches, 18% to Google Maps, and 10% to TikTok, with social media as a group at 26%. If your clients skew younger, your Instagram and TikTok content needs to show real work, real staff, and real results, within your profession's advertising rules.

Your website closes the decision

MyAdvice's survey found that 59% of respondents said the About Us page is the most important part of a med spa website. Clients want to know who will treat them. Include provider names, credentials, photos, and a short description of your approach.

Keep every listing consistent

BrightLocal found that 56% of consumers often or always check that a business's information is accurate and consistent across sources, and 67% read reviews after a local search. Check your name, address, phone number, hours, and services on Google, Yelp, your website, and your social profiles.

Make it easy to take the next step

Put a booking link and a phone number where clients are already looking: your Google profile, your Instagram bio, and the top of your website. Fewer taps between interest and appointment means more bookings.

An illustrative example

A prospective client sees a short video on Instagram, searches the clinic name on Google, reads recent reviews, and opens the website to find the provider's background. At each step, she is asking the same question: can I trust these people? If the Instagram handle, the Google listing, and the website use different names, hours, or photos, doubt creeps in at the exact moment you want confidence.

Common mistakes to avoid

Using different business names across platforms, hiding provider information, posting results that break advertising rules for your field, and forgetting to link the booking page. Keep the path from first look to appointment short and consistent.

A simple plan for the next 30 days

Week one, check every listing and profile for the same name, address, phone, hours, and services. Week two, update your website About page with provider names, credentials, and a short description of your approach. Week three, post a short, rules compliant piece of content on the social platform your clients use, with a clear booking link. Week four, ask five new clients how they found you and compare the answers with your plan. Adjust where you place your effort based on what clients actually say. Keep a simple checklist so that every new provider, service, or price change updates all of these places at once.

Try this first

Search your business name on a phone. Compare your Google profile, website, and Instagram. Fix any difference in hours, services, or phone number today.
