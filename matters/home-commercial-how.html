How to turn a lead into a booked job: answer fast, quote clearly, follow up

By ContentMatter · 3 min read · Published November 4, 2026

A call or a form is only a request. It becomes a job when someone answers quickly, quotes clearly, and follows up when the customer goes quiet. Most of that is routine, not talent.

Speed comes first

Harvard Business Review published research by James Oldroyd, Kristina McElheran, and David Elkington on how companies handle online sales queries. Their conclusion was direct: most companies are not responding nearly fast enough. That research was about online queries in general, not home services specifically, but a customer asking for a quote is usually asking two or three companies at once.

For calls, the gap is easy to see. Invoca reports that 27% of calls to home services businesses go unanswered, and fewer than 3% of callers who reach voicemail leave a message. A quick callback or text is often the only second chance you get.

Reply on every channel

Customers reach out by phone, web form, text, and messages from your Google listing. Decide who owns each channel and how fast it should be answered, then write that down. Google's Local Services Ads treat messages, voicemails, and booking requests as leads, so a message in your inbox deserves the same urgency as a ringing phone.

Quote in plain terms

A good quote names the work, the price or price range, what is and is not included, and the next step. Customers compare several companies, and a clear quote is easier to say yes to than a vague one. If you need to see the job first, say when you can come and whether the visit costs anything.

Follow up on a schedule

Most customers do not say no. They go quiet. Follow up the same day, again after two or three days, and once more after a week. Keep each message short, mention the job, and make replying easy. Stop after the last one so you stay helpful instead of pushy.

Close the loop in your numbers

Mark every lead as booked, lost, or pending, and note why when one is lost. After a month you can see how many leads you win, how long they take, and which source sends the best jobs. This is the same job log we describe in our post on what to track.

An illustrative example

A cleaning company gets ten quote requests in a week. Four get a reply the same day, three the next day, and three after the weekend. The next week it sends a first reply within an hour during business hours and a follow up two days later. If more of those leads book, the routine is working. If not, the quotes may be the problem, and that is worth knowing too. This is a made up scenario to show the method, not a result to expect.

Common mistakes to avoid

Replying with only a price, letting messages sit over the weekend, following up once and stopping, losing leads in a shared inbox that nobody owns, and judging ad results without checking how many leads were actually answered.

A simple plan for the next 30 days

Week one, list your last ten leads and note how long each waited for a first reply and whether it booked. Week two, write a short callback text, a quote template, and two follow up messages. Week three, assign an owner for every channel and start the follow up schedule. Week four, compare your booked rate with week one and adjust the weakest step. Keep the log going so the improvement is visible.

Try this first

Look at your last ten leads. Note how long each waited for a first reply and whether it booked. That gap is your starting point.
