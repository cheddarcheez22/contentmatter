Why every missed call is a lost job

By ContentMatter · 3 min read · Published September 2, 2026 · Updated September 30, 2026

Calls are how most home service jobs begin, and a call that goes unanswered rarely comes back. Invoca reports that 27% of calls to home services businesses are not answered, so the leak is bigger than most owners assume.

The call comes before the job

Invoca's 2022 Buyer Experience Report found that 62% of consumers call before making a purchase. For a plumber, electrician, or HVAC company, that call is the front door to the sale. Your ads, your listings, and your website all exist to make one thing happen: a customer dials your number.

That makes the phone the single most valuable piece of equipment in the business. If it goes unanswered, everything upstream of it (ad spend, reviews, a good website) is wasted.

Voicemail is not a safety net

It is tempting to think a missed call will leave a message and you will call back later. Invoca's platform data suggests otherwise: fewer than 3% of callers who reach voicemail leave a message. Most simply call the next business on the list.

The same Invoca research reports that 76% of consumers say they would stop doing business with a company after a single bad experience. An unanswered call is often a customer's first experience of your company, and it is a poor one.

Put a number on it

You do not need an industry average to see the cost. Use your own figures: missed calls in a month, multiplied by the share of calls that usually book, multiplied by your average job value. Even a conservative booking rate usually produces a number worth acting on.

Practical ways to answer more calls

Answer live during business hours, and decide who covers lunch, jobs in progress, and evenings. Use call tracking so every call is logged with its source, including the ones nobody answered. Send an automatic text to missed callers so they hear from you within minutes. Finally, check that your ads only run when someone can pick up. Google Ads lets you limit ads to the days and hours you can answer inquiries, which keeps you from paying for clicks that end in a ringing phone.

An illustrative example

Picture a plumbing company running search ads on a busy Monday. The ads work, the phone rings, and two technicians are on jobs while the office person is on another line. Three callers reach voicemail, hang up, and call the next company in the results. None of those lost calls show up in a report, because the ad platform only sees the click. The business concludes the ads are underperforming when the real problem is answering capacity.

Common mistakes to avoid

Treating a missed call as a minor inconvenience, running ads outside staffed hours, relying on voicemail to catch leads, and judging ad performance without checking how many calls were actually answered. Each one makes good marketing look worse than it is.

A simple plan for the next 30 days

Week one, measure: log every call for seven days and mark each as answered or missed. Week two, close the biggest gap, whether that is lunch coverage, a second line, or an answering service for overflow. Week three, add an automatic text reply for missed calls and set your ad schedule to match staffed hours. Week four, compare the answered rate against week one and see how many more jobs were booked. Keep the log going after that. A phone that is watched every week tends to get answered more often, and the improvement is easy to see.

Try this first

Pull last month's call log and count three things: total calls, answered calls, and missed calls. That ratio is your starting point for everything else.
