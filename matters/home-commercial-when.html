When to run ads: seasons, weather, and emergency demand

By ContentMatter · 3 min read · Published September 8, 2026 · Updated September 30, 2026

Service demand spikes with heat waves, cold snaps, and burst pipes, not with the calendar. Match your budget, hours, and bids to the moments people actually need you, and make sure someone can answer when the ads run.

Know your local climate

The U.S. Energy Information Administration's 2020 Residential Energy Consumption Survey found that 88% of U.S. households use air conditioning. That figure varies widely by region: 93% in the South, 73% in the West, and only 49% in the Pacific Coast marine climate area. If you serve coastal and inland neighborhoods, expect cooling demand to look different in each, and to jump during heat waves rather than build evenly.

Let your own data set the schedule

Google Ads lets you choose the days and hours your ads can appear and set bid adjustments for specific times. By default, campaigns run all day. Google's own guidance is to check the Day and Hour performance data, find the periods that produce results, and limit or adjust ads to match. A weekly review of that report takes a few minutes and often changes where the money goes.

Plan for emergencies honestly

Emergency searches happen at night and on weekends. If you offer after hours service, make sure the phone is answered then, or the ads should be off. If you do not, schedule ads for the hours when your team can respond. Paying for an evening click that goes to voicemail is wasted spend.

Protect your budget in peak season

Local Services Ads stop showing once your monthly maximum is reached. Check your spend early in the month during busy stretches, and raise the limit before a heat wave or cold snap, not after the first day of missed leads.

An illustrative example

A cooling company notices that its calls double during the first heat wave of the year, then drop the following week. Because the ads ran evenly all summer, the budget was thin during the spike and heavy in the calm. By watching weather forecasts and the Day and Hour report, the owner can raise bids and budget for the days before a heat wave and trim them afterward.

Common mistakes to avoid

Running the same budget all year, leaving ads on when no one can answer, forgetting that monthly limits can end a campaign early, and treating a slow week as a failure when it simply followed a mild one. Look at several weeks and several seasons before changing the plan.

A simple plan for the next 30 days

Week one, export Day and Hour data for the past 90 days and mark your best and worst time blocks. Week two, set an ad schedule and bid adjustments that follow those results and your staffing. Week three, decide how you will handle after hours emergency calls and make sure that choice is reflected in your ads. Week four, check your monthly spend against the cap and set a reminder to review it before the next busy stretch. Then repeat each month. Small adjustments applied consistently usually beat a big rebuild once a year.

Try this first

Open the Day and Hour report in Google Ads for the last 90 days. Mark the three strongest time blocks and the three weakest, then adjust your schedule or bids to match.
